Miami Beach, Florida Miami-Dade Commission Watch conrad@conradgelber.com

Most people never see a county commission meeting. The agendas run hundreds of pages, the votes happen on a Tuesday morning, and the decisions reach further into daily life than almost anything that happens in Tallahassee or Washington. In August 2026 I co-founded a nonprofit to fix that for Miami-Dade, and I have been building the coverage, the volunteer bench, and the software behind it ever since.

The Brechner Report, the University of Florida journalism school's freedom-of-information newsletter, has covered Miami-Dade Commission Watch and Florida County Commission Watch.

The rest of my work sits in the same territory: government transparency, international politics, and the question of how institutions earn the trust they ask for.

Work

2025 – present

Miami-Dade Commission Watch

Co-founder and executive director

Daniel Llobet and I spent 2025 decoding hundred-page Board of County Commissioners agendas by hand before any website existed. The site launched on August 1, 2026, and we incorporated in Florida on August 17, 2026; 501(c)(3) status pending. We publish plain-language summaries of every meeting, with each item linked back to its source document, in English, Spanish, and Haitian Creole. The site has a commissioner directory by district, an elections page, a guide to speaking at public comment, and a district lookup tool I built so residents can find their commissioner by address. The editorial policy is nonpartisan: no endorsements, no spin, and every claim sourced from public records. Six student editorials ran in the first month. Submissions are open to any Miami-Dade student, and the student keeps the byline.

Things I've built

2026

miamidadecommissionwatch.com

HTML and JavaScript, Vercel, Supabase, Cloudflare

The public site for Miami-Dade Commission Watch. It carries plain-language summaries of every county commission meeting in English, Spanish, and Haitian Creole, and a directory of all 13 commissioners by district. I also set up all of the project's email infrastructure. Residents use it to follow what the Board of County Commissioners decided without reading the agenda themselves.

2026

MDCW district lookup

JavaScript, Census Bureau geocoder, Vercel function

Residents enter an address and get back their county commission district and commissioner. The address goes through a small serverless proxy to the Census Bureau geocoder, and the browser matches the point against the county's district boundaries. It answers the first question most people have before public comment: who represents me?

2026

flcountycommissionwatch.com

Next.js, Vercel, Supabase, Cloudflare

The statewide version of the Miami-Dade model. Each Florida county will get its own subdomain and participant guide, and a student lead will claim the county and run its site with a local team.

2026

Next.js, Vercel

A site I build and maintain for my dad's guided fishing charters and ecotours in the Everglades. The business has not opened yet, so a coming-soon page sits in front of the tours, the gallery, and the trip request forms until it does.

This site and the globe game

HTML, CSS, and JavaScript on Cloudflare Pages

No framework and no build step on deploy. The fonts, images, and map data are all served from this domain, and the pages make no requests anywhere else. The globe game at /play/ asks you to drop a pin on capitals, second cities, and remote towns.

Where this is going

I want to join the Foreign Service, most likely on the political or economic track, and I intend to spend a career in it. The work I do now is the same work at a smaller scale: read the documents nobody reads, figure out what a government is actually doing rather than what it says it is doing, and write it down clearly enough that it changes what someone else decides.

I am fluent in Spanish, which I use every day in Miami and expect to use in the Foreign Service.

If that path closes, political and geopolitical risk analysis is the version of the job that exists in the private sector, and I am building the technical skills to do it well.
